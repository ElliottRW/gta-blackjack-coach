How the tests work

Card mix. Every card you've recorded (yours, the dealer's and other players') is counted and compared with a real deck, where each rank is 1 in 13 and 10-value cards are 4 in 13. The percentage is the chance a fair deal would look at least this uneven: under 5% is worth watching, under 1% is suspicious.

Dealer results. How often the dealer finished on each total, compared with the exact math for the upcards they actually showed. Dealer blackjacks are listed separately. Rounds where you'd already bust or had blackjack are left out: the dealer doesn't draw then, so their total only gets recorded if their first two cards made 17 or more.

Decks. Cards in one round come out of the same shoe. With one deck the exact same card can never appear twice in a round; with more decks it gets a little more likely, and most likely if every card is drawn at random. The tab works out which deck count best explains every round. Suits make this about ten times more sensitive.

Between rounds. If GTA keeps dealing from the same shoe, cards from one round are missing from the next. This compares that with a fresh shuffle each round, looking back up to 10 rounds played within 5 minutes of each other. It only knows the cards you recorded, so it errs towards "not clear yet".

Same table. Rounds you both marked "Same table as a friend", logged within 2 minutes of each other with the same dealer upcard, count as one round, and each card is only counted once.
